# Chapters

2026-09-27. Row 17 of the August feature-gap list (since replaced by `docs/research/landscape.md`)
— "AI titles, summaries, chapters" — was held
because the Rust recorder does it by uploading, and nothing here leaves the machine. The on-device model
(`FoundationModels.SystemLanguageModel`, macOS 26) removes that reason. This document settles
what a chapter is, where it lives, what the model is allowed to decide, and how it gets into the
exported file.

**The competitor matrix of the time said chapters already existed** — "✓ Mark track". There is no
mark track in the code; nothing in `Core/Sources` or `Soredemo/` names one. That row is corrected
in the same commit as this document.

## Questions, and what was measured before answering them

**Can an exported MP4 carry chapters that other software reads?** Measured, 2026-09-27, macOS 27,
a 64×64 six-second throwaway:

| Attempt | Written | AVFoundation reads back | ffprobe reads back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| timed-metadata track, `chap` association from video | yes, `meta` track present | **0 chapters**, no chapter locales | — |
| `tx3g` text track, `chap` association, language `und` | yes | 3 of 3, with `bestMatchingPreferredLanguages: ["und"]`; **0 with `["en"]`** | 3 of 3, titles and times exact, including `錄影` and `✓` |

The same result for `.mov`. The text track is written disabled (`marksOutputTrackAsEnabled =
false`), or a player shows the chapter names as a subtitle.

**Three things the two-second fixture could not show**, found by a ten-second export with audio
(`chaptersAcrossALongRecordingWithAudioAreAllWritten`) — the same trap `MP4Exporter` records for
audio, whose interleaving window is about two seconds:

| Tried | What happened | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| append each chapter when the frames reach its start | stalled at frame 163 (2.7 s) | the writer will not let the video run past its window beyond the chapter track's last sample, which was the one at 0 s; reproduced in a spike with no audio, stalling at frame 167 |
| append chapters whenever their input is ready, times in nanoseconds | `-17771` on the chapter starting at 7.1 s | the track takes its timescale from the samples and MP4 stores times in 32 bits: 7.1 × 10⁹ is past 4.29 × 10⁹. Every chapter in the short fixture was under two seconds |
| the same, in milliseconds | all four chapters, times exact | — |

`mediaDataLocation` (`.beforeMainMediaDataNotInterleaved`, and macOS 26's
`.sparselyInterleavedWithMainMediaData`) was tried against the nanosecond version and failed
the same way, which is how the overflow was separated from the interleaving: it is not set.

An `encd` box declaring the title UTF-8 was in the first version, on the belief that a reader
would otherwise take the bytes for MacRoman. Measured without it, `錄影` and `✓` came back intact
through both readers, so it is not written.

**Is the model usable here, and how much fits?** `SystemLanguageModel.default` reported
`available`, `contextSize 4096` tokens; a twelve-sentence request with a `@Generable` answer took
5.7 s. 4096 tokens is roughly ten minutes of narration, so a long transcript is sent in windows.

## Decisions

**A chapter is a title and a moment in the RECORDING's time** — the clock `TextTrack`,
`AnnotationTrack` and the captions use — so cutting and reordering move it for free.
`ChapterTrack.outputChapters(through:outputDuration:)` is the one conversion, and it reports
rather than hides what it did:

- a chapter whose moment survives lands where that moment now plays;
- **a chapter whose moment was cut moves to the first surviving moment after it** — its content
  still exists, only its first seconds went — and is listed as moved;
- a chapter with nothing surviving after it is dropped, and listed as dropped;
- two chapters that land on the same output time are both kept and the collision is listed: two
  derivations disagreeing say so.

**The model chooses a sentence, never a time.** It is shown numbered sentences and answers with
a sentence number and a title. The time is that sentence's `start` from the transcript, so a
proposal cannot name a moment nobody said anything at. `ChapterProposals.validate` refuses — each
with a reason a person can read — a number outside the transcript, a number not after the
previous one, an empty title, a sentence the edit has cut entirely, and a sentence within ten
seconds of an existing chapter. **Every proposal carries its evidence**: the sentence number and
the sentence's text, shown beside it.

**Automation proposes; the person adds.** Proposals are a list beside the chapters with Add and
Dismiss on each and Add All for the list. **An added proposal becomes an ordinary chapter** —
nothing is overwritten by asking again, because proposals never replace, only offer (the
producer-value rule: supply a chapter no proposal could produce, ask again, and it is still
there). An added chapter keeps its origin — "suggested from sentence 14" — until its title is
edited, which makes it the person's.

**The model is `SystemLanguageModel.default` and nothing else.** The SDK also offers
`PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`; it sends the prompt off the machine and is not used. A source
test forbids naming it. When the on-device model is unavailable, the button says why — not
eligible, Apple Intelligence off, model still downloading — and chapters can still be added by
hand.

**The exported file carries them** when there are any: MP4 and MOV, not GIF (the format has no
such thing). They go through the EXPORT's edit, so exporting a marked section carries only the
chapters inside it. In the render tier the chapter's text is `PlacedChapter.name`, not `title`:
there `title` means the accessibility title an observation log carries, which Gate 2 forbids the
render path to read, and a line of source has no types to tell the two apart. **And a list can be copied** — `0:00 Title` lines — for a video description. Copying
is the person's act, and nothing is sent anywhere; it goes through `Clipboard`, which is now the
one line in the app that names the pasteboard (the export's copy moved there too). The list says when it would not be accepted
as chapters by the common host that reads them (first not at 0:00, fewer than three, one shorter
than ten seconds); it does not silently fix them.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Chapters in output time.** Every cut would leave them pointing at different content.
- **Letting the model return timestamps.** Its numbers would need checking against the
  transcript anyway; making it choose a sentence removes the class of error instead.
- **Silence-based chapters without a model.** A pause is where a person breathed, not where the
  subject changed — `Transcript`'s own header makes the same point about segments.
- **Timed-metadata chapter track.** Written without complaint and not read back; see above.

## Platform premises (D6b)

- A `tx3g` text track with a `chap` association is read as chapters by AVFoundation and ffprobe.
- Chapters in a track tagged `und` are returned for `["und"]` and not for `["en"]`.
- `SystemLanguageModel.default.contextSize` is 4096 on this machine.
